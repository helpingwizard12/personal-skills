# Learn by Scenario

Teach the topic in `$ARGUMENTS` (or the concept the user is stuck on) using the method below. The learner should *feel the problem* before they hear the solution.

## Method (follow in order)

1. **Pick a role for the learner.** Choose the person who *uses or benefits from* the concept — usually the one making the decision (founder, shop owner, investor, engineer on-call). If the user named a role ("as if I'm a founder…"), use that. Make it vivid and concrete: "You run a startup earning $70M/year."

2. **Give them a problem first.** A real pain the concept solves. Don't name the concept yet.
   - Good: "You want a top engineer. Google pays $600k. You can afford $250k."
   - Bad: "ESOPs are a form of equity compensation…"

3. **Reveal the solution as the natural answer to that problem.** Now introduce the concept, in one plain sentence.

4. **Walk through real numbers.** Use simple round numbers and show the math step by step. Include both outcomes: when it works and when it doesn't.

5. **Show trade-offs from the role's view.** A short table: what they gain, what it costs them.

6. **Name the jargon last.** Introduce each technical term only once the idea behind it is already clear (e.g., "that $5 is called the *strike price*"). Give acronyms their full form.

7. **One-line summary.** A single sentence the learner could repeat to a friend.

8. **Offer the next angle.** End by offering one follow-up: the *other side's* perspective (employee vs founder, buyer vs seller), the hidden catch, or the next-level concept.

## Style rules

- Short sentences, everyday words. No paragraph longer than 3 lines.
- Analogies from daily life (shops, kitchens, cricket, rent) beat abstract definitions.
- Use the learner's context if known (country, currency, job).
- Max ~400 words for the first pass. Depth comes from follow-ups, not one giant answer.
- If the learner says "didn't understand", don't repeat — switch to a **smaller, more personal scenario** with even simpler numbers.

## Check understanding (optional, if the learner wants)

Ask one scenario question that requires applying the idea, not reciting it:
"Your share price drops to $3. Should your engineer exercise their options? Why?"
